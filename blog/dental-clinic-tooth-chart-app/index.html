A cavity, a crown and a missing tooth, all on one colour coded mouth chart

Say a patient has a cavity, a crown from last year, and a tooth that is missing. On a paper form, that becomes a drawing of a mouth and a few codes like 1-7, 1-6 and 4-8, written in whoever's handwriting was nearest.

The codes are not the problem. Every dentist reads them. The trouble starts after the visit. A code on a page tells you where a tooth is. It does not show you what is going on with it at a glance, and it does not tell you how many patients had a cavity in the lower left this year, unless someone opens every file to count.

A tooth has a place, a problem and a treatment

They belong together, and a mouth is the one picture everybody already understands. So when we built a staff app for a dental clinic, we put a chart of the whole mouth at the centre. It has the same four quadrants as the paper form, with teeth numbered 1 to 8 from the middle outwards.

Then we colour coded the conditions and treatments, from a cavity to a crown, a bridge, an implant or a missing tooth, so one glance at the chart tells you what each tooth has or needs. A solid outline means it is already there. A dashed outline means treatment is still needed.

Staff do not type a tooth number. They tap.

Two ways to mark, because staff think in two ways

Some days the work goes tooth by tooth. Other days it goes condition by condition. The chart works both ways, so nobody has to change how they think.

What the doctor sees afterwards

Because every mark is tied to a real tooth and a real condition, the record is more than a drawing. Open a patient and the mouth is there as it stands today, with what is already done and what is still pending side by side.

Across all patients, the same marks turn into answers. Which problems come up most, which teeth, which age groups. Nobody counted anything by hand.

It also means the clinic's records no longer depend on one person who knows how the register works. A new receptionist can use the chart on day one, because there is nothing to learn that the paper form had not already taught.
